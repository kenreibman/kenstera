## The client

TheDavidImage is the online portfolio of photographer David Reibman. His work is panoramic fine-art photography, built around a format he calls "mollage," where several frames come together into one piece. The website is where that work lives: galleries to browse, works to buy, films to watch, and the story behind it all.

## The challenge

The portfolio ran on Squarespace. For a site like this, the content is the whole point, and it is not a simple blog or a brochure. It is nine galleries of images, a shop where individual works are listed with titles and prices, films, an about page and a way to get in touch.

Moving that to a new platform meant three things at once:

- **Nothing could be lost.** Every gallery, work and page had to come across intact.
- **The images had to stay front and center.** Photography needs a site that stays out of its way.
- **The content needed a better home.** The site needed a content system built around how a photographer's work is actually organized, not a generic template.

## Our approach

We treated it as a custom CMS project rather than a template swap. We rebuilt the site on Payload CMS, with the galleries, shop works, films and pages all managed as content in the CMS, and a custom front end designed around the work.

## What we built

- **A gallery for each body of work.** Nine galleries, each on its own page with the work shown large and the number of images listed.
- **A shop of individual works.** Each work has its own page, with its title and price.
- **A films section.** The films sit on the home page alongside the rest of the portfolio.
- **An about page.** The story of how the work came about, in David's own words.
- **A contact page.** A clear way to get in touch.

## The tech stack

- **[Payload CMS](https://payloadcms.com/).** The custom CMS that now manages the site's content.
- **Next.js.** The framework behind the custom front end.
- **Squarespace.** The platform the site was migrated from.

## What changed

The portfolio moved off Squarespace and onto a site and CMS built specifically for it. The galleries, shop works, films and about page now live in Payload, in a structure that matches how the work is organized.
